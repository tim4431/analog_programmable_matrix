LC1 CIRCUIT SIMULATION SCOPE
===========================

Simulator: LTspice 24.1.3 (Windows).
35 completed benches: 13 row tests and 22 individual feedback-loop tests.
Each recorded result includes its circuit SHA-256. results.json is rebuilt
from per-bench records; evidence_matches_netlist must be true for every case.
Raw simulation waveforms and simulator logs are local generated files and
are excluded from Git; rerun the benches to reproduce the report plots.

VENDOR MODELS
ADA4899-1, OPA2810, THS3091 and OPAx197 vendor macromodels are used.
They model amplifier behavior, not a complete manufactured multichannel PCB.
OPA2810 channels use separate instances and do not model interchannel coupling.
OPA4197 trim loops use the family OPAx197 single-channel model.
Model provenance is recorded; download scripts keep licensed models out of Git.

DAC7811 APPROXIMATION
The TI-downloadable DAC7811 TINA model is encrypted and is not used in
LTspice. A behavioral current source implements q*Vref/Rdac, with an
8/10/12 kohm matched reference/feedback resistance, output resistance,
code-dependent capacitance allowance, and a separate first-order bandwidth
pole. The nominal pole is 10 MHz, based on the datasheet typical multiplying
bandwidth. This is an engineering approximation, not a validated vendor model
or a guaranteed minimum bandwidth. A 5 MHz pole stress is included and fails
the 1% magnitude target at 1 MHz (2.15% droop).

The modeled full coefficient is q=0.93552, yielding approximately unity
loaded transfer after the analog gain reserve. Actual firmware uses integer
12-bit codes and measured positive/negative full-code gains. INL, DNL,
reference feedthrough, digital glitches and silicon process distributions
are not comprehensively modeled. Codes 0.1, 0.5 and near full range are
included as linear transfer checks, not as an exhaustive code sweep.

SWITCH APPROXIMATION
TMUX6136 uses static selected/unselected resistances and terminal capacitance,
with 120 ohm nominal and a conservative 245 ohm resistance stress. Board
supplies are +/-15 V. Charge injection, switching transients, leakage over
temperature and powered-off behavior require measurements.

PARASITIC ASSUMPTIONS
Nominal summing-node DAC capacitance: 5 pF device + 5 pF stray.
Stress: 10 pF device + 10 pF stray.
Nominal feedback compensation: 1.0 pF component + 0.3 pF stray.
Loop sweep: total feedback capacitance 1.1 pF and 1.5 pF.
These are assumed bounds, not extracted PCB parasitics. The real board must
be checked for ringing/oscillation and frequency response at all 16 cells.

The modeled input stage drives all four row reference loads, including both
selected and unselected switch capacitances. Four independent weighted
paths feed a representative row. Four coherent input tones/steps test full
summation. The complete four-output board is not an EM/crosstalk simulation.

OUTPUT LOAD
The THS3091 uses 1210 ohm feedback, 402 ohm gain resistor, +/-15 V rails,
49.9 ohm series source termination and external 50 ohm load. Capacitive-load
checks use 20 pF and 1 nF in parallel with the external 50 ohm termination.
The 1 nF case is a stress model, not a substitute for a transmission-line
model of arbitrary cables and mismatched loads. High-impedance loads can
see roughly twice the specified loaded voltage.

ANALYSIS
AC gain is normalized to the low-frequency magnitude. Thus reported 1 MHz
droop is amplitude flatness after scalar gain calibration, not absolute
uncalibrated accuracy or complex transfer error. No deep cancellation or
phase-matching specification is claimed.
Loop gain is measured through an AC series injection preserving DC bias.
Phase margin is reported at downward unity crossings.
Transient solver uses Gear integration, reltol=0.001; runs reach 12 us and
include both rising and falling full-sum transitions. Model numerical
damping and ideal sources can differ from measured high-frequency ringing.
DC sweeps cover 0 to +1 V and 0 to -1 V with both coefficient signs.

REQUIRED HARDWARE WORK
Gain/offset calibration; response through 1 MHz for each sign/path;
zero-code feedthrough; simultaneous full-sum output swing; spectral noise;
cross-channel cancellation if needed; output cable/loading behavior;
power/USB sequencing and mute transients; power integrity and thermal soak
inside the enclosure. Current-limited first power-up and insulated heatsinks
remain required. Simulation does not establish production yield or safety.
